The problem

A company must decide which depots to open (at most p) and how to route vehicles from them to customers. In the latency location-routing problem (LLRP) the objective is not distance but total latency: the sum of customer arrival times. Because latency accumulates along a route, moving one customer early shifts the arrival times of everyone after it, so every move is evaluated by recomputing the latency of the affected routes.

Objective

Minimise the total latency:

min⁡  ∑j∈JTj\min\;\sum_{j\in J} T_jminj∈J∑​Tj​

where TjT_jTj​ is the arrival time of customer jjj, measured from the start of its depot's route.

Constraints

  • Depot limit. At most p depots are open.
  • Single visit. Every customer is served by exactly one route.
  • Vehicle capacity. The demand on one route is at most the vehicle capacity Q.
  • Fleet size. At most Nv routes are used.
  • Open routes. A route starts at an open depot and ends at its last customer. The return edge to the depot does not contribute to the latency objective, so it is not modelled (Ngueveu et al., 2010; Osorio-Mora et al., 2023). The solver's latency and distance computations follow the same convention.

Local search operators

A local search repeatedly scans a neighbourhood: the set of solutions reachable with one move of a given operator. It either applies the best improving move (best improvement, the default) or the first one it finds (first improvement). It stops when no operator in the selected set improves the solution.

Each card gives the move, its origin, and the closest counterpart in the cited papers. Our block operators (oropt, swap22 and the like) generalise the single-customer moves; they are our extension, not taken from the cited papers.

✓ means the paper describes this move. ≈ means the closest counterpart, with a different definition. — means the paper does not describe it. MJL16 uses Swap, Insertion and 2-Opt in its memetic local search, and Swap, Relocation and 2-Opt in its granular search. NG22 uses Swap, Insertion and 2-opt inside routes, with Exchange and Relocation between routes. OM23 and OM23a describe the same operator set: insertion, swap, 2-opt (with the two-route crossing), arc-swap, shift2-1, and the depot moves of their random phase or perturbations. They differ in the metaheuristic, a simulated annealing with VND in OM23 and an iterated local search in OM23a.
