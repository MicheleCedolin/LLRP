1

The problem in one paragraph

In the latency location-routing problem (LLRP), we choose at most p depots to open, assign vehicles to them and route customers. The objective is the total latency: the sum of customer arrival times, not total travel distance. Because latency accumulates along a route, moving one customer early in a route shifts the arrival times of everyone after it. Every move is therefore evaluated by recomputing the latency of the affected routes.

Costs in this application are total latency. Gap to best known solution (BKS) = 100 (cost - BKS) / BKS.

2

How a local search uses the operators

A local search repeatedly scans a neighbourhood: the set of solutions reachable with one move of a given operator. It either applies the best improving move (best improvement, the default) or the first one it finds (first improvement). It stops when no operator in the selected set improves the solution.

Which paper uses which operator

Five papers are compared, listed by year. MJL16 Moshref-Javadi and Lee (2016); NG22 Nucamendi-Guillén et al. (2022); OM23 Osorio-Mora et al. (2023, ITOR); OM23a Osorio-Mora et al. (2023, COR); Z24 Zou et al. (2024). Each column is also a preset in the solver's local search section, and All papers is the union of the operators that at least one paper describes exactly.

✓ means the paper describes this move. ≈ means the closest counterpart, with a different definition. — means the paper does not describe it. MJL16 uses Swap, Insertion and 2-Opt in its memetic local search, and Swap, Relocation and 2-Opt in its granular search. NG22 uses Swap, Insertion and 2-opt inside routes, with Exchange and Relocation between routes. OM23 and OM23a describe the same operator set: insertion, swap, 2-opt (with the two-route crossing), arc-swap, shift2-1, and the depot moves of their random phase or perturbations. They differ in the metaheuristic, a simulated annealing with VND in OM23 and an iterated local search in OM23a.

Z24: oropt2 is a different version of N4.
