1

The problem in one paragraph

In the latency location-routing problem (LLRP), we choose at most p depots to open, assign vehicles to them and route customers. The objective is the total latency: the sum of customer arrival times, not total travel distance. Because latency accumulates along a route, moving one customer early in a route shifts the arrival times of everyone after it. Every move is therefore evaluated by recomputing the latency of the affected routes.

Costs in this application are total latency. Gap to best known solution (BKS) = 100 (cost - BKS) / BKS.

2

How a local search uses the operators

A local search repeatedly scans a neighbourhood: the set of solutions reachable with one move of a given operator. It either applies the best improving move (best improvement, the default) or the first one it finds (first improvement). It stops when no operator in the selected set improves the solution. The GRASP-RVND component of llrp_fast applies the operators in random variable-neighbourhood order; the metaheuristics apply the same descent inside their own search loop.

Open directions from the literature

  • Capacitated depots, which none of the cited LLRP papers treat in full (Moshref-Javadi and Lee, 2016; Nucamendi-Guillén et al., 2022).
  • Route duration limits, and demand or travel-time uncertainty (Nucamendi-Guillén et al., 2022).
  • Multi-objective trade-offs, for example latency against open depot cost (Moshref-Javadi and Lee, 2016).
  • Time-window LLRP and extensions to CCVRP and FLP (Osorio-Mora et al., 2023).
  • Learning-guided neighbourhood ordering, which Zou et al. (2024) apply with Q-learning; this is directly comparable to the RVND ordering in llrp_fast.

Code and data availability: the 76 benchmark instances are public (Tuzun-Burke, Prodhon, Barreto). None of the five cited LLRP papers releases solver code; some state that data is available on request.
